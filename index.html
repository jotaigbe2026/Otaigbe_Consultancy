Why People Call

When a Materials Problem Becomes a Business Problem

These are the situations where an independent technical view usually pays for itself — and where being wrong is expensive.

Failures, returns and warranty exposure

Parts cracking, warping, discolouring or leaking in service, with returns and customer complaints escalating behind them.

Material substitutions and cost-reduction decisions

An “equivalent” grade, a supplier change or a cost-down target on a part that is currently working.

Rising scrap and unstable processing

Scrap climbing, quality drifting between shifts or lots, and a process that has never settled into a stable window.

Delayed development and uncertain scale-up

A programme slipping on materials questions, or a formulation that works at pilot scale and not in production.

Product-liability, IP and manufacturing disputes

A technical question at the centre of a claim, where the answer has to hold up under scrutiny.

How We Work

From Business Impact to a Practical Path Forward

The investigation is scoped around the decision that is waiting on it, which is what keeps it proportionate.

1

Clarify the business impact

Define what is happening, what it is costing and what decision needs to be made.

2

Frame the technical question

3

Build the right investigation

Prioritise analysis, testing, trials or technical review around that decision.

4

Deliver a practical path forward

Findings, risks, recommendations and next steps, in clear language.
