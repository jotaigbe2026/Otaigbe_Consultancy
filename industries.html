Industries

Sectors where this practice can point to real experience — in the material systems, the process areas and the failure modes that define them. Listed here only where that is true.

Discuss Your Challenge

⚛ Polymers & Plastics

The core discipline of the practice: a PhD and forty years in polymer science and engineering, and the material class behind most of the failures, substitutions and process problems that arrive here.

  • Engineering plastics, polyolefins, polyamides, polyesters and polycarbonates, styrenics, polyurethanes and thermosets.
  • Degradation and ageing — hydrolysis, oxidation, UV attack, environmental stress cracking.
  • Formulation and additive-package effects on performance and processability.
  • Biodegradable and bio-derived plastics, and the end-of-life questions that come with them.

🪨 Composites

Fibre-reinforced and hybrid systems, where performance depends as much on the interface and the process as on the constituents.

  • Carbon and aramid fibre composites, FRP and GRP.
  • Nanocomposites and organic–inorganic hybrid systems — a direct research specialism.
  • Fibre–matrix debonding, delamination and interface failure.
  • Cellulose-nanocrystal and other bio-derived reinforcement systems.

⚙ Manufacturing & Processing

Where a material meets a machine. Most quality and scrap problems are decided here rather than in the specification.

  • Extrusion and injection moulding — window definition, defects, dimensional stability.
  • Compounding and formulation — dispersion, distribution, batch consistency.
  • Coatings and films — adhesion, cure, barrier performance.
  • Rheology and polymer reaction engineering as the link between formulation and processability.

📦 Consumer Products

High-volume plastic products, packaging and consumer goods, where a small per-part materials decision multiplies into a large commercial one.

  • Consumer plastic products and consumer materials science and engineering.
  • Packaging materials, barrier performance and sustainable alternatives.
  • Paints, thin films and surface finish durability.
  • Sports and recreation products; computers and wearable electronics housings.

🚗 Automotive & Transportation

Materials substitution and lightweighting decisions with warranty exposure attached, plus the aerospace-grade requirements that increasingly follow them.

  • Metal-to-polymer and metal-to-composite substitution and its failure modes.
  • Under-bonnet and battery-system polymers — thermal, chemical and electrical service conditions.
  • Aircraft and aerospace structures and components.
  • Durability, creep and fatigue over a design life measured in years, not test cycles.

⚡ Energy & Oil/Gas

Long-service-life assets in aggressive environments, where a materials failure is rarely cheap and frequently contested.

  • Chemicals and petrochemicals; oil and gas service environments.
  • Petroleum underground storage tanks (USTs) and flexible piping — a specific area of experience.
  • Protective and corrosion-resistant coatings.
  • Materials for renewable-energy systems and the sustainability constraints attached to them.
