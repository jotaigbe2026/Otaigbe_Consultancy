The Executive’s 12-Question Materials Failure & Manufacturing Cost Checklist

A short guide for deciding whether a product failure, quality problem, material change or process issue requires independent technical review — or whether your own team should keep it.

Who this is for

Operations directors, engineering and quality leaders, and general managers who have a materials or manufacturing problem in front of them and have to decide what to do about it — usually without a materials specialist in the building.

It is not a sales document. Several of the twelve questions are designed to establish that a problem does not need outside help, which is the correct answer more often than a consultancy’s website normally admits.

What is this actually costing?

Scrap, rework, returns, warranty reserve, expedited freight, held inventory and the engineering hours already spent. Most problems are escalated on the visible number and turn out to be several times larger.

Has the evidence been preserved?

Failed parts left as found, retains from the same material lot, process data from the period, and photographs. Almost nothing else in an investigation is recoverable once this is lost.

Did anything change before it started?

Resin lot, supplier, additive package, regrind practice, tooling, drying, an operator, a shift pattern, a season. The date the problem started is usually the most informative fact available.

Is this one failure mode or several?

A single mechanism and three unrelated ones look identical on a scrap report and need completely different responses.

Could this become a legal matter?

If there is any prospect of a product-liability, IP or supply dispute, the investigation should be scoped for that from the start — work repurposed as evidence later is weaker.
